- **[2026-10-20] D-SERVICE-AUTH: organization and cards verify the identity access token; roles come from identity memberships** (approved by the founder in chat, option "verify token + look up role"): The organization and cards services never verified the caller. Their `TenantAuthGuard` copied `X-User-Id`/`X-User-Role`/`X-Tenant-Id` from the request into `req.user`, so (a) the real dashboard, which sends a token and tenant but no role header, got 403 on every admin action, and (b) anyone could send `X-User-Role: owner` and act as owner of any company. Now: `RequestAuthenticator` (`packages/shared/src/request-auth.ts`) verifies the HS256 access token locally (constant-time signature check, `alg`, `type: 'access'`, expiry) and resolves the caller's role in `X-Tenant-Id` via identity's new service-key endpoint `GET /internal/memberships/role`, cached 60 s (so a removed membership stops working within a minute; fails closed if identity is unreachable). Header-only identity is accepted only when `NODE_ENV=test` (existing suites rely on it). Organization and cards refuse to start in production without a real `JWT_SECRET` equal to identity's. Session revocation (logout) is not checked by these services; access tokens are short-lived, so a revoked session stays usable there until expiry. Dashboard QR/standee downloads now use an authenticated fetch instead of `window.open`, which cannot send the token.

- **[2026-10-22] D-UI-REDESIGN: "Span" design language replaces arches and curved motifs across OneBridge Grid**:
  - **Decision ID**: `D-UI-REDESIGN`
  - **Decision**: The arch and keystone crown motif from UI/UX Spec v1.0 and F12 are superseded workspace-wide by the "Span" design language per founder directive:
    1. *Geometric foundation*: Straight lines and modular grid only. No arches, domes, circles, or curves anywhere in cards, shell, dialogs, or marketing components.
    2. *Span line*: A signature charcoal hairline (`#393536`, or `#FAF7F2` in dark mode) with a small square node at each end (`--node-size: 4px` / `5px`). Used for section dividers, progress indication (fills from left to right), and the share-back connection animation.
    3. *Keystone tag*: The verified company mark becomes a small trapezoid chip, retaining the bridge keystone symbolism with clean polygonal geometry.
    4. *Grid architecture*: Modular content tiles separated by 1px Sand (`#E3D9C8`) hairlines. Nesting is restricted to a maximum of 2 levels.
    5. *Corner radii*: Strict scale of 6px for interactive controls (buttons, inputs), 10px for panels and cards, and 14px for bottom sheets and modals.
    6. *Business ID nameplate*: `OB-T15EH7` displayed in monospace on a Sand plate (`#EFE8DC`) as the signature anchor on the dashboard shell, visitor card, and printed collateral.
    7. *Colour discipline*: Bridge Orange (`#F68713`) is strictly a fill colour, never a text colour. Orange buttons carry charcoal text (`#393536`). Text or icons on light backgrounds use Deep Orange (`#A04E08`). Charcoal gives structure; Charcoal Deep (`#1E1E1E`) forms the dark mode canvas.
    8. *Typography & copy*: Strict sentence case across all headings, buttons, tabs, labels, and feedback messages. 12/14/16/20/24/32/40 scale; weights 400, 600, 700 only.
    9. *Scope boundary*: Step 1–3 backend and domain rules remain unchanged. Rebuild applies to frontend presentation layers (`packages/ui-kit`, `apps/web-public`, `apps/web-app`) following the D0 approval gate.

## D-N08: real-time chat implementation

- Date: 2026-10-27
- New `services/messaging` (port 4007, `/svc/messaging`). Global collections `conversations`, `messages` and read models `messaging_company_refs`, `messaging_seat_refs`, `messaging_follow_refs` (from org, seat and follow events; ADR-0003); tenant-scoped `conversation_notes`. Approved in the collection guard test.
- Message text is encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM: company conversations (member to company, card) with the company's tenant key, others (member to member, company to company) with a per-conversation key; the AAD binds each ciphertext to its message. Unsend deletes the ciphertext. Events carry IDs only (`conversation.started`, `message.sent`, `conversation.assigned`); text is never logged.
- Request rule: a new conversation with a member becomes a request unless that member follows the sender, or the message answers their requirement or call request (N07, N11). Messages to a company are enquiries and go to its inbox with no request step. Until accepted the sender has one message, without links; replying accepts; ignoring is silent (the sender keeps seeing "pending").
- Limits (sample numbers, plan section 5): new message requests per month are 5 for members (env `MEMBER_REQUESTS_PER_MONTH`) and 5, 50 or 200 for Free, Starter or Business companies (Enterprise unlimited); plus 30 new conversations a day per account (env `DAILY_NEW_CONVERSATION_CAP`). The spec's "per day by plan" became monthly, as the plan table states.
- Company inbox: anyone in the company can act as it with `X-Act-As-Company`; Owner and Admins see everything, other seats see conversations assigned to their seat. Only Owner and Admins start conversations for the company and assign them. Card conversations are assigned to the card's seat. Assignment is to a seat, not a person, so when a seat passes to a new person (F16) the conversations go with it; a seat that stops being active hands its conversations back to the unassigned queue. Notes, status and assignment are never shown to the other side; for company to company conversations the receiving company's inbox handles them.
- Realtime: WebSocket at `/realtime?ticket=` with a 60-second single-use ticket from `POST /realtime/ticket`, so the access token never goes in a URL; the Origin must be the app's. Frames: message.new, message.updated, typing, read, presence, conversation.updated. Messages are sent over HTTP with a `clientMessageId` so retries never duplicate; the app keeps messages typed offline in an outbox ("Not sent yet") and sends them when the connection is back. Fan-out is in-process on one node (tested with 1,000 sockets, delivery under a second); Redis pub/sub is needed before a second node. The single-app server and demo API proxy WebSocket upgrades.
- Edit and unsend window: 15 minutes (the proposed answer to the N08 open question). Retention: company conversations stay while the company exists (to be deleted with it under F28).
- Not yet: attachments and malware scanning (no file storage yet; the API refuses attachment IDs with `attachments_unavailable`), link previews, voice notes, searching message text (text is encrypted; search covers the other side's name), blocking (N09), hiding read receipts and online status (N10), offline notifications (F22 consumes `message.sent`), routing to a branch contact (F26 routing; new enquiries wait unassigned for Owner and Admins), and creating a lead from a first message (cards will consume `conversation.started`).


## D-OTP-RANDOM: one-time codes are random

- Date: 2026-10-27
- Sign-in codes (phone and email) were always `123456`. `OtpService` now draws each code with `crypto.randomInt` (6 digits, zero-padded).
- The fixed code `123456` remains only when `ENABLE_FAKE_OTP=true` (dev, demo server, e2e launcher, tests), passed as `fixedOtpCode`. The env loader refuses `ENABLE_FAKE_OTP` in production, so a production build cannot issue the fixed code.
- The newest challenge for a phone now wins a `createdAt` tie (`_id` tie-break in Mongo, insertion order in memory); before, two requests in the same millisecond could return the older, already-used code.

## D-AUTH-EMAIL: Google and email + password sign-in

- Date: 2026-10-27 (founder: Google + email/password with verification code, Docker Compose VPS, real customers)
- D1 Email goes out over SMTP (one adapter fits ZeptoMail, SES SMTP or any provider); a console adapter is for development and tests and refuses to start in production.
- D2 Codes come from `EMAIL_FROM` (no-reply@onebridgecard.com) with reply-to `EMAIL_REPLY_TO` (support@onebridgecard.com).
- D3 Phone sign-in is off in production and on in development and tests, through `AUTH_METHODS`.
- D4 An email code is asked at sign-up, at password reset, and at sign-in from a new device or after 30 days without a code on that device (httpOnly `ob_device` cookie, stored hashed in `known_devices`). Google sign-in needs no code.
- D5 Passwords are hashed with scrypt from `node:crypto` (N=2^15, r=8, p=1).
- D6 Google and email accounts with the same email link automatically only when both emails are verified; otherwise the account owner confirms with an emailed code.
- D11 Invite claim (F14) uses email in production.
- New global collections `email_codes` (TTL on `expiresAt`) and `known_devices`, approved in the collection guard test. Pending sign-ups keep the name and password hash on the code record, so an abandoned sign-up leaves no account.
- Phone remains a profile and card field; identity events carry IDs only.
